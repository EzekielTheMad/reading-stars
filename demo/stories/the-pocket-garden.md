# The Pocket Garden

An original Reading Stars demo story. Read this story before taking its quiz.

Nia wanted to grow a garden, but her family lived above a busy shop. There was no yard. There was only a narrow windowsill and a pot with a crack near its rim.

“A garden does not have to be big,” her grandfather said. He gave her three bean seeds.

Nia filled the pot with soil. She pushed the seeds down gently and placed the pot in the sunlight. Every morning, she gave it a small drink of water.

On the first day, nothing happened. On the second day, nothing happened either. Nia almost dug up the seeds to see if they were broken.

“Roots grow before leaves,” her grandfather told her. “Some work happens where we cannot see it.”

On the fifth morning, a pale green loop poked through the soil. Nia called it Pip.

A week later, Pip leaned toward the window. Nia thought it was falling over. She tied it tightly to a pencil with string.

By evening, the stem looked bent where the string touched it. Nia untied the string. She asked her grandfather what to do.

“Give it support and room to grow,” he said.

They made a loose loop instead. Nia turned the pot a little each morning so the plant would not always lean in the same direction.

Soon the other two seeds sprouted. The windowsill held three small plants, three pencil posts, and three loose loops of string.

Nia's neighbor asked why she called such a tiny pot a garden.

“Because I care for what grows here,” Nia said.

When the first bean appeared, Nia did not pick it at once. She watched it grow for several more days. This time, waiting felt like part of the work.
