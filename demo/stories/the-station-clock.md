# The Station Clock

An original Reading Stars demo story. Read this story before taking its quiz.

Every Saturday, Arun helped his aunt open the little railway museum. His job was to wind the old station clock. Visitors loved its deep, slow chime.

One morning, the clock stopped at nine seventeen. Arun wound it, but the hands did not move. He tried again. His aunt gently took the key.

“More force is not always more help,” she said. “Let us find the cause first.”

They placed a sign beside the clock: RESTING TODAY. Then they opened the museum on time.

Arun wanted to hide behind the ticket desk. He had told his friend Ivo that the clock always worked. Now he was sure Ivo would laugh.

Instead, Ivo asked, “Can we look for clues?”

The two friends examined the outside without touching the mechanism. Arun noticed a scratch near the lower door. Ivo noticed that the clock case leaned slightly to the left.

“Someone must have broken it,” Arun said.

His aunt asked what their observations proved. Arun looked again. A scratch could be old. A leaning case did not tell them who had moved it, or whether moving it had caused the clock to stop.

They checked a photograph from the previous week. The scratch was already there. The case, however, had stood straight.

Under one foot of the case, Ivo found a folded visitor map. During yesterday's floor cleaning, a helper had moved the clock and accidentally left it resting on the map.

Their aunt called the museum's clock repairer. She described the stopped hands and the uneven case. The repairer asked her to leave the winding key alone until he arrived.

While they waited, Arun and Ivo made a paper clock for the welcome desk. Its hands pointed to the next tour time. It could not chime, but it helped visitors find their group.

The repairer arrived after lunch. He steadied the case, checked the pendulum, and restarted the clock. He explained that an uneven case can interrupt a pendulum's regular swing. The extra winding had not solved that problem.

Arun replaced RESTING TODAY with a new card: PLEASE ASK BEFORE MOVING THIS CLOCK.

“Was your promise about the clock wrong?” Ivo asked.

“It was too certain,” Arun said. “Next time I will say that it usually works.”

At closing time, the clock gave six slow chimes. Arun listened to every one. Then he put the winding key in its drawer and wrote a note about the map in the museum's care book.
